At 390px the columns are already uneven: “Transactions” holds 65px and the other five get 62px, against 75px each in the five-slot bar above. At 360px they are 65 and 56, against 69. How the bar moves on each kind of debt2026-09-17

His question of 2026-09-17: on a card, unlike a loan, paying gives the room back — so what does the bar do? They fill for opposite reasons, and each is the natural one for its type. On a credit card the bar is the limit in use: a purchase pushes it up, a payment pulls it back down, and at $0 owed it is empty with the whole limit available again. On a loan the bar is what you have paid off: it only ever grows, because you cannot re-borrow what you repaid — his own words — and it is full the day the loan is finished. The line under each says which it is, so the bar is never read alone. What it costs, and it is why this is drawn rather than described: two bars on the same list fill for opposite reasons, so a card at 31% and a loan at 30% mean different things. The alternative — one rule for both, the bar always being the debt that is left — was what the first draft did, and it made the loan start full and empty as you paid, which reads backwards.
