Shared

Expenses split with other people, and who has paid you back. A shared group is one outing or one trip, so it has a date range and never a month. The lead figure everywhere is what still counts as yours: an expense is born entirely yours and only falls when somebody actually pays, which is why nothing here can be read as money you already have.

Undoing a payment2026-09-21

A payment recorded by mistake is undone, never balanced with a second one: a payment the other way is a real event, and using it to fix a typo leaves two movements that never happened. The sheet says what reaches further than the row it was opened from — the movement goes with it, what counts as yours goes back up in the month each expense happened, and everything else that person has paid is imputed again over what is still open. What it does not touch: no expense leaves the group and a write-off stays a write-off. Reached from here and from the movement's own detail (transactions.html#payment-detail).

Undo this payment?
$300,000 goes back to being owed. Undoing a payment is not a refund: what was recorded stops having happened, and no new movement is written.

The movement it wrote goes with it, because that money is the payment's. What counts as yours goes back up on the expenses it had lowered, each in the month that expense happened, and each one keeps the change in its history.

Nothing else is undone: no expense leaves its group, and what was written off stays written off. Everything else Beto Cano has paid is imputed again over what is still open, oldest first.

A line somebody else paid2026-09-22

T-139, and the third way into Add expense. A line another participant paid is not a movement of yours, so this is deliberately not the transaction form: there is no account, no category and no budget to ask about, and a form that asked for them would be asking about a movement that does not exist. It asks for the four things such a line is — what it was, when, how much, and who paid it — and inherits the group's split without asking, exactly like the other two doors. It writes one thing and not two, and the sheet says so, and says when that stops being true: the day you settle with Ana it becomes your expense, dated that line and in a category you choose then. Who paid is never you — that is what the other two doors are — so a group of one does not draw the door at all. A description is required here, where the transaction form lets it go: this line has no category to borrow a name from, and neither will the expense it turns into.
