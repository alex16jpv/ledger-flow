# Decision log

Every decision that shaped the UI, newest first. This is the one file in the specification that keeps
the tracker's codes (`F-nn`, `P-nn`, `W-nn`, `D-nn`): it is the audit trail, and it is what ties a
screen back to the row that asked for it in `PROGRESO.md`. The screens themselves are named after what
they are, never after a ticket.

| 2026-09-22 | **T-129 · an invitation is answered inside Shared, and it never tells anybody whether an address has an account** (the owner's four answers of 2026-09-22). Each group is **its own invitation**, addressed to the email of the person in it, so nobody ever lands in a group they did not accept; it **waits 30 days**; a group in **another currency** cannot be joined, and only the invited person is told why; and inviting with an address nobody has verified is **a known risk he accepted**: whoever registers with somebody else's address receives what is sent to it, which is why the person who invited sees `Joined` and can `Stop sharing` at any time. The invitations sit above both faces of Shared and are found from a brand dot on More and a count on the Shared row and in the sidebar — the notifications inbox is not scheduled, and the day it exists these signs become its. Answering, inviting and withdrawing need a connection: each one is about somebody else, and only the server can say whether the invitation still stands.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

| 2026-09-20 | **T-111 · a payment belongs to the person, not to the expense it landed on.** What a payment covers is imputed **oldest expense first**, which is why somebody can read `Partially paid` in a group and `Paid` on its first expense. The decision that makes that rule safe to store is the second half: **deleting an expense, or editing its split, re-imputes every payment over what is left**, and each expense it touches records it in its history. Nothing a payment did is ever undone by editing an expense. The alternative considered and not taken was spreading each payment pro-rata over the person's open shares, which is order-independent and needs no explanation, but no single expense ever reads `Paid` and the figures stop being round; if the odd-peso rule teaches anything it is that round figures are worth protecting.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
